Diffusion-DICE: In-Sample Diffusion Guidance for Offline Reinforcement Learning

DICE learns the ratio between the optimal and the dataset distributions. Diffusion-DICE reads this ratio as a transformation of the behavior policy: the optimal policy's score is the behavior score plus a guidance term that we learn in-sample. It guides a diffusion behavior model toward high-value in-support actions, then selects the best of a few candidates.

Diffusion policies exploit critic errors

Diffusion models fit multi-modal behavior well, which makes them attractive for offline RL. Existing methods turn them into policies in one of two ways:

  • Guide-based (QGPO) learns a guidance term from critic values on actions generated by the diffusion behavior model.
  • Select-based (IDQL, SfBC) draws many candidates from the diffusion behavior model and resamples them by critic value.

Both query the critic on generated actions. A diffusion model can still produce out-of-distribution actions, especially with frequent sampling, and the critic tends to overestimate exactly those, so the guidance or the selection is drawn toward them.

The fix is to use the critic only on dataset actions during guidance, and only on a few well-guided candidates during selection.

DICE as a policy transformation

DICE methods solve an \(f\)-divergence-regularized LP form of RL through its dual, which uses only \((s,a,s')\) samples from the dataset:

$$\min_V\ \mathbb{E}_{(s,a)\sim d^{\mathcal D}}\Big[(1-\gamma)V(s) + \alpha f^*\big([Q(s,a)-V(s)]/\alpha\big)\Big],$$ $$\min_Q\ \mathbb{E}_{(s,a,s')\sim d^{\mathcal D}}\Big[\big(r(s,a)+\gamma V(s')-Q(s,a)\big)^2\Big].$$

The solution gives the optimal stationary distribution ratio, \(w^*(s,a) = d^*(s,a)/d^{\mathcal D}(s,a) = \max\big\{0,(f')^{-1}\big(Q^*(s,a)-V^*(s)\big)\big\}\). Since a policy is its stationary distribution normalized over actions, the same ratio transforms the behavior policy into the optimal one:

$$\pi^*(a|s) \;\propto\; w^*(s,a)\;\pi^{\mathcal D}(a|s).$$

For a diffusion model, all we need is the score of \(\pi^*\) along the reverse process, and it splits into two terms:

$$\nabla_{a_t}\log\pi^*_t(a_t|s) \;=\; \underbrace{\nabla_{a_t}\log\pi^{\mathcal D}_t(a_t|s)}_{\text{diffusion behavior model}} \;+\; \underbrace{{\color{#5B2C8A}\nabla_{a_t}\log\mathbb{E}_{a_0\sim\pi^{\mathcal D}(a_0|a_t,s)}\big[w^*(s,a_0)\big]}}_{\text{guidance}} .$$

Previous DICE methods extract a unimodal Gaussian policy from \(w^*\); here the diffusion model keeps every mode of \(\pi^*\).

Guide: in-sample guidance learning

The guidance term is a log of a conditional expectation, which is intractable directly. For any non-negative \(f\), however, \(\min_y \mathbb{E}_x\big[f(x)e^{-y} + y\big]\) is convex and minimized by \(y^*=\log\mathbb{E}_x[f(x)]\). Applying this along the forward diffusion process gives the In-sample Guidance Learning (IGL) objective for a guidance network \(g_\theta\):

$$\min_\theta\ \mathbb{E}_{t\sim\mathcal U(0,T)}\,\mathbb{E}_{{\color{#5B2C8A}a\sim\pi^{\mathcal D}(a|s)}}\,\mathbb{E}_{a_t\sim p(a_t|a_0)}\Big[w^*(s,a)\,e^{-g_\theta(s,a_t,t)} + g_\theta(s,a_t,t)\Big],$$

whose optimum satisfies \(\nabla_{a_t} g_{\theta^*}(s,a_t,t) = \nabla_{a_t}\log\mathbb{E}_{\pi^{\mathcal D}(a_0|a_t,s)}\big[w^*(s,a_0)\big]\).

  • In-sample. \(w^*\) is evaluated only on dataset actions, so the guidance never relies on value predictions for generated actions.
  • Unbiased and convex. One dataset action per state suffices for an unbiased estimate, and the loss is convex in \(g_\theta\) because \(w^*\ge 0\).

A piecewise f-divergence

The log in the guidance is undefined where \(w^*=0\), so \((f')^{-1}\) must stay positive, while \(f^*\) should stay numerically tame. Reverse KL satisfies the first condition and Pearson \(\chi^2\) the second; we join them:

$$f(x) = \begin{cases}(x-1)^2 & x\ge 1\\ x\log x - x + 1 & 0\le x<1\end{cases}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad (f')^{-1}(x) = \begin{cases}x/2+1 & x\ge 0\\ e^{x} & x<0\end{cases},\qquad f^*(x) = \begin{cases}x^2/4+x & x\ge 0\\ e^{x}-1 & x<0\end{cases}.$$

The ratio is now strictly positive everywhere, and \(f^*\) is at most quadratic.

Then select

The behavior constraint makes \(\pi^*\) multi-modal, so following its score can still land in a local mode. We sample a few candidates from the guided model and keep the one with the highest DICE critic value:

$$\pi(s) = \arg\max_{a\,\in\,\{a^1_0,\dots,a^K_0\}\,\sim\,\pi^*(\cdot|s)}\ Q^*(s,a).$$

Because the candidates already come from in-support, high-value regions, a small \(K\) is enough, and few critic queries leave little room to exploit its errors. On AntMaze, Diffusion-DICE uses 1 to 8 candidates where IDQL uses 32.

Diffusion-DICE
  1. Pretrain a diffusion behavior model \(\epsilon_\psi\) on the dataset.
  2. Train \(Q\) and \(V\) with the DICE losses and the piecewise \(f\)-divergence.
  3. Train the guidance network \(g_\theta\) with IGL on dataset actions.
  4. At test time, sample \(K\) actions with score \(-\epsilon_\psi(a_t,s,t)/\sigma_t + \nabla_{a_t} g_\theta(s,a_t,t)\) and execute the one with the highest \(Q\).

Sanity check: a 2-D bandit

Dataset actions follow a standard normal distribution restricted to an annulus; the true reward peaks on the outer ring. The diffusion behavior model and the learned reward both fit the data well but make errors where there is no data, and the learned reward is too high in the empty center.

Diffusion-DICE reaches a mean of 88 on MuJoCo and AntMaze against 83 for QGPO, the strongest diffusion baseline, and is best on all four Kitchen and Adroit tasks, e.g. pen-cloned (57.3 → 83.8). The gap to O-DICE (81), a strong DICE method that extracts a Gaussian policy, reflects how multi-modal DICE's optimal policy is.

Guiding also lowers overestimation in practice. With the same critic and behavior model, guide-then-select reaches higher returns than selecting from behavior samples, while its selected actions have lower predicted Q-values.

Takeaways

  1. The DICE ratio \(w^*\) transforms the behavior policy into the optimal one, and the optimal score is the behavior score plus a guidance term.
  2. The guidance term can be learned in-sample with a convex objective, using critic values on dataset actions only.
  3. Guide, then select: guiding first means a few candidates suffice, which keeps the critic away from the actions it overestimates.
